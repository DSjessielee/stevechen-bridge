Part I

Two Over One Bidding System

01

Opening Bids

A tour of every opening bid in the bidding box, from 1♣ up to 5♠. Learn what unusual openings like 5♥ and 5♠ show, and how vulnerability and seat position shape your decisions.

preempts vulnerability seat position
02

Responding

The cornerstone of 2/1: establishing game-forcing auctions when responder bids a new suit at the 2-level. Covers forcing 1NT, Stayman, transfers, support bids, and responses to preempts.

2/1 game force Stayman transfers
03

Opener's Rebid

After the first round of exchange, the opener refines their hand description into minimum, medium, or maximum. Includes a clear-eyed look at reverse bids — often misused, now demystified.

reverses hand evaluation limited hands
04

Responder's Rebid

With opener's shape and strength now in view, responder zooms in on the final contract level and strain. Introduces New Minor Forcing and 4th-Suit Forcing — indispensable system tools.

NMF 4th-suit forcing game decisions
05

Overcalls & Advances

Bridge is a competitive sport — you don't have to give opponents a free run. Learn when and how to overcall, the role of the takeout double, and how to stay disciplined about suit quality and vulnerability.

takeout double aggression penalties
06

Responding After RHO Overcalled

Does the overcall change what your bids mean? Systematically covered by categorizing RHO's action: NT overcall, suit overcall, double, or preempt — a complete map for each scenario.

negative double cuebids suit vs NT
07

Advanced Topics

Modern competitive bidding: the many faces of double, artificial 2NT, two-suited overcalls, balancing, and passed-hand bidding. Emphasis on current expert practice and firm partnership agreements.

Michaels unusual 2NT balancing
08

Slam Bidding

4NT is the most abused convention in bridge. Learn when to use it — and when not to. Control bids, non-serious 3NT, and the Last Train convention give your slam bidding the precision it deserves.

control bids RKCB Last Train non-serious 3NT

Part II

Card Play Technique

09

The Fundamentals

Power of honors, trick promotion, types of finesses, and entry management — the building blocks every declarer must have.

finesses entries honors
10

Suit Combination

Suit distributions, the principle of restricted choice, entry management, and safety plays to protect your contract.

restricted choice safety play
11

Playing Notrump Contracts

Count winners, build a plan to develop more, and deploy hold-up and avoidance plays. Blocking and unblocking to free entries when you need them most.

hold-up avoidance blocking
12

Declaring Suit Contracts

Count losers and systematically eliminate them — ruffing, discarding, or finessing. When to draw trumps and when to delay, plus cross-ruffing and the elegant dummy reversal.

loser count cross-ruff dummy reversal
13

End Plays

The most common advanced play technique — found in every serious text on declarer play. More accessible than most think: a two-stage process of elimination followed by throw-in. Covered through extensive worked examples.

elimination throw-in advanced
14

Other Advanced Techniques

Bath coup, trump coup, scissors coup, Morton's fork, and deceptive play to keep the defense guessing.

coups deception
15

Introduction to Squeezes

Simple squeeze, double squeeze, strip squeeze. Rectifying the count and the Vienna coup to set them up.

squeeze Vienna coup

Part III

Defense Play

16

The Fundamentals

Leads and signals, the rule of 11, second and third hand plays, and the BOSTON framework for opening leader discipline.

opening lead signals rule of 11
17

Defending Notrump

Defense against NT is often a race. Return partner's suit, read card combinations, deploy hold-up play, and count — obsessively — to reconstruct declarer's hand.

card reading hold-up counting
18

Defense at Suit Contracts

Draw inferences from the bidding, identify the dummy's role, and form a plan: aggressive, passive, ruff-cutting, or stopping suit establishment. Count through every trick.

active defense passive defense ruff control
19

Defensive Techniques

Trump promotion, uppercut, unblocking and overtaking plays, hold-ups, and the specialist coups: Descapells, Merrimac, and Crocodile.

trump promotion uppercut coups
20

Partnership Defense

Interpreting signals, negative inference, discarding, returning and shifting suits, and protecting your partner. The focus is partnership: not just what your signals say, but how to think in your partner's shoes at the table.

signals inference partnership discards
